# Script Generation Playbook

`anansi-mood-weaver` creates visual script support from the structured brief. Story Weaver owns the final narrative route and script spine. Mood Weaver makes sure the words, sound atmosphere, pacing, and visual world do not fight each other.

## Script Job

Before writing, classify the script:

- brand-building
- sales activation
- founder / B2B
- hero film
- social short
- event promo
- absurd/parody product launch
- atmospheric brand film

Do not write one generic script that tries to do every job. If the narrative route is unresolved, hand that question to Story Weaver instead of solving the whole story inside Mood Weaver.

## Script Inputs

Use:

1. structured brief
2. the creative lead lens notes
3. job classification
4. story route or Story Weaver handoff when available
5. brand/video effectiveness requirements
6. viral/platform requirements when relevant
7. mood system

## Route Before Script

Generate up to 3 visual/script-support routes only when the brief is unresolved. If Brief Agent or Story Weaver already selected a direction, produce one route with controlled visual variations. Each route should include:

- route name
- one-line concept
- emotional promise
- visual world
- script approach
- why it works
- risk
- the creative lead fit

Recommend one route, then write visual script support from that route. Do not replace Story Weaver's script spine.

## Script Rules

- Land the hook immediately.
- Do not open with a logo unless the logo is integrated into the moment.
- Make the first image and first line agree.
- Keep the CTA single and concrete for sales activation.
- Let the moodboard determine light, material, and pacing.
- Let the script determine the emotional sequence and information order.
- If captions are required, write them as part of the script, not after it.
- If sound matters, specify music/voice/silence/sonic cue.
- If the job requires final copy or narrative structure, route to Story Weaver.

## the creative lead Rules

- Avoid over-explaining symbolism.
- Let atmosphere carry meaning when the brief is poetic or spatial.
- Keep emotional language specific and physical.
- Reject lines that sound like generic luxury, generic theater marketing, or generic tech launch copy unless parody is intentional.
